General Start-up & Shut-down Procedures Section 4.0 — do this before every experiment
Purpose Prepare the FM-24 unit and the hydraulic bench so that the test section is full of water, free of trapped air, and showing manometer levels inside the visible range. Every experiment below assumes these steps have been completed.
4.1 · General start-up
  1. Ensure the clear acrylic test section is installed with the converging section upstream and that both unions are hand-tight. To dismantle, withdraw the total-head probe fully first.
  2. Locate the apparatus on the flat top of the bench and level it using the four adjustable feet (check with a spirit level).
  3. Fill the volumetric tank of the hydraulic bench until approximately 90 % full.
  4. Connect the flexible inlet tube using the quick-release coupling.
  5. Connect a flexible hose to the outlet and direct it into the channel / measuring tank.
  6. Partially open the outlet flow control valve on the FM-24.
  7. Fully close the bench flow control valve V1, then switch on the pump.
  8. Gradually open V1 and let the piping fill until all air has been expelled from the system.
  9. Check for trapped bubbles in the manometer tubes. Press the air-bleed valve at the top of the manometer board and let fluid + air escape until every tube is clean. (In this simulator: click Bleed air.)
  10. Increase the flow rate, then close the discharge valve until the maximum measurable flow is reached. Water levels on the manometer board should now be clearly visible.
  11. If the levels are too low, open V1 to raise the static pressure; if too high, open the outlet control valve to lower it. The tapping next to the outlet valve is the set-up datum.
  12. The actual flow of water is measured with the volumetric tank and a stopwatch.
4.2 · General shut-down
  1. Close the water supply valve and the venturi discharge valve.
  2. Turn off the water supply pump.
  3. Drain off water from the unit when not in use.
Maintenance & safety (Section 6.0). Drain all water when not in use and store the apparatus properly. A manometer tube that fills slowly indicates a blocked tapping — disconnect the flexible tube and blow through. Do not expose the unit to shock or stress. Always wear protective clothing, shoes, helmet and goggles during laboratory sessions, and only run the experiment once you fully understand the unit and the procedures.
Experiment 1 — Procedure Section 5.1 · 8 steps
Objective To determine the discharge coefficient, Cd, of the venturi meter by comparing the actual (volumetric) flow rate with the ideal flow rate calculated from the measured head difference.
  1. Perform the General Start-up Procedures (Section 4.1) — use the checklist above or the Run start-up checklist button.
  2. Withdraw the hypodermic (total-head) probe from the test section by slacking the gland nut and sliding it clear. Click Withdraw probe. The probe must not obstruct the flow for this test.
  3. Adjust the discharge valve to the maximum measurable flow rate. This is achieved when tubes A and C give the maximum observable water-head difference (design flow rate of the venturi = 20 LPM).
  4. After the level stabilises, measure the water flow rate using the volumetric method (fill the 5 L tank, time it) and record the manometer readings hA … hF. Click Record data point.
  5. Repeat step 4 with at least three decreasing flow rates by regulating the venturi discharge valve. Aim for a good spread of points (e.g. ≈ 15, 12, 9, 6 LPM).
  6. Obtain the actual flow rate, Qa, from the volumetric flow measurement method. The app does this automatically when you record.
  7. Calculate the ideal flow rate, Qi, from the head difference between hA and hC using Equation 3.18. The app computes this for you.
  8. Plot Qa versus Qi and obtain the discharge coefficient, Cd, which is the slope of the best-fit line (forced through the origin).
Qi = A₂ · [1 − (A₂/A₁)²]−1/2 · √(2g · Δh)   … where Δh = hA − hC  (Eq. 3.18)
Cd = Qa / Qi   … slope of the Qa vs Qi plot  (Eq. 3.20)
A₁ = inlet area 5.309 × 10⁻⁴ m²  ·  A₂ = throat area 2.011 × 10⁻⁴ m²  ·  g = 9.81 m/s²
Expected result. For a well-machined venturi the discharge coefficient normally lies between 0.90 and 0.99. A value far outside this range means trapped air, a leaking coupling, or a blocked pressure tapping.
Before you record: the probe must be withdrawn (step 2), the pump running, all air bled from the manometer tubes, and the flow steady. The simulator will refuse to record if the probe is still inserted.
Set a stable flow, then record. Flow rate is measured volumetrically (5 L).

Experiment 2 — Procedure Section 5.2 · 7 steps
Objective To measure the flow rate with the venturi meter and verify the result against the actual volumetric flow rate, using the discharge coefficient obtained in Experiment 1.
  1. Perform the General Start-up Procedures (Section 4.1).
  2. Withdraw the hypodermic probe from the test section — click Withdraw probe. The static pressure taps alone must be used for this measurement.
  3. Adjust the discharge valve to a high measurable flow rate.
  4. After the level stabilises, measure the water flow rate using the volumetric method and record the manometer readings. Click Record data point.
  5. Repeat step 4 with three other decreasing flow rates by regulating the venturi discharge valve.
  6. Calculate the venturi meter flow rate of each data set by applying the discharge coefficient obtained in Experiment 1  (Qcalc = Cd × Qi).
  7. Compare the volumetric flow rate with the venturi meter flow rate and compute the percentage error for each point.
Qcalc = Cd × Qi
Error (%) = | (Qcalc − Qa) / Qa | × 100
Dependency. This experiment uses the Cd determined in Experiment 1. If you have not recorded any points there yet, the simulator falls back to the nominal value Cd = 0.936 — run Experiment 1 first for a proper result.

Experiment 3 — Procedure Section 5.3 · 10 steps
Objective To demonstrate Bernoulli's Theorem by showing that the total head (static + velocity head) remains essentially constant along the venturi, while the static head falls where the velocity rises.
  1. Perform the General Start-up Procedures (Section 4.1).
  2. Check that all manometer tubings are properly connected to the corresponding pressure taps and are air-bubble free. If not, press Bleed air.
  3. Adjust the discharge valve to a high measurable flow rate.
  4. After the level stabilises, measure the water flow rate using the volumetric method.
  5. Gently slide the hypodermic tube (total-head probe) connected to manometer #H so that its end reaches the cross section of the venturi at #A. Wait for the reading to settle, then note manometers #H and #A.
    Manometer #H reads the total (stagnation) head h* — static + velocity head — because the probe faces the flow and brings it to rest (zero velocity).
    Manometer #A reads only the static pressure head hi, because the wall tapping does not obstruct the flow.
  6. Repeat step 5 for the other cross sections #B, #C, #D, #E and #F.
  7. Repeat steps 3 to 6 with three other decreasing flow rates by regulating the venturi discharge valve.
  8. Calculate the velocity ViB using Bernoulli's equation: V_iB = √(2·g·(h* − h_i))
  9. Calculate the velocity ViC using the continuity equation: V_iC = Q_av / A_i
  10. Determine the difference between the two calculated velocities for each cross section and comment on the agreement.
Bernoulli:   h* = hi + v²/2g = constant   (Eq. 3.9)
ViB = √(2 · g · (h* − hi) / 1000)   [m/s]
ViC = Qav / (60000 · Ai)   [m/s]
Ai = π·Di²/4  ·  cross-section diameters from Appendix C
One-click traverse. Instead of sliding the probe by hand six times, press ⟶ Traverse probe & record all sections. The simulator sweeps the probe from A to F, waits for each manometer to settle, and fills the table for the current flow rate. Then change the flow rate and traverse again for a second data set.
Before you record: the probe must be inserted (this experiment needs it), the pump running, and all air bled from the manometer tubes.

Interpretation. The static head hi falls sharply at the throat (higher velocity) and recovers in the diverging cone. The total head h* stays nearly constant, decreasing only slowly because of wall friction — exactly what Bernoulli's theorem predicts for an ideal, frictionless, incompressible flow along a streamline.
